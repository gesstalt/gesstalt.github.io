Project Description

Optical Music Recognition (OMR) is a field of research that focuses on using machine learning, computer vision, and immersive XR technologies to automatically recognize, decode, and interact with musical scores from scanned or digital document images into machine-readable formats (such as MusicXML or MIDI).

Decoding complex musical notations typically involves multi-stage processing steps:

  1. Low-level Analysis: Detecting and recognizing base musical primitives, including staff lines, individual noteheads, clefs, accidentals, and rests.
  2. Mid-level Analysis: Establishing spatial and structural relationships among musical symbols (e.g., mapping notes to correct staff lines and measuring pitches).
  3. High-level Analysis: Synthesizing structural semantics, semantic formatting, and full digital score generation and immersive XR workspace translation.

Datasets & Heterogeneous Alignment

Training a unified OMR model for immersive environments faces a core domain gap: Western staff notation encodes pitch vertically, while percussion notation encodes timbre. To resolve this, we combine DeepScores V2 with our proposed SWARA-Percussion dataset using a unified harmonization pipeline.

MUSICA: Music Score Image Corpus for Analysis

Description: MUSICA provides thousands of synthetically generated and real-world scanned manuscript images containing diverse font styles, spacing, and multi-instrument arrangements.

SWARA-Percussion Dataset

Existing datasets exclude percussion notations and fail to capture shape-based notehead variants (such as '$\times$' for cymbals). SWARA-Percussion is a dense-object detection benchmark containing 1,000 single-page drum notation scores with 38,000 annotated symbols.
